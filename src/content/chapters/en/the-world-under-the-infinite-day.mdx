## A Bright Night

The deafening blare of the alarm jolts Olawale Adebayo awake.

He opens his eyes and stares at the :term[omni-screen]{id="omni-screen"}. It tells him that it is 10\:00 PM, and that his next shift begins in 45 minutes.

"Shit," he thinks. "I can't be late again."

With an awkward leap, he gets out of the small bed, with its white sheets and pale blue pillowcase, and crosses to the tiny toilet directly in front of it. The 108 sq ft apartment is white, sterile, silent. The only sound is the constant hum of the ventilation system. Even the air smells neutral.

But that does not bother Olawale. Deep down, perhaps it even comforts him.

He pulls off his sleeping jumpsuit and sits down. A brief reprieve, a moment to finish waking up.

As he relieves himself, he watches the movement outside through the window, across the New York :term[Arcology]{id="arcology"}: a myriad of people walking, bumping into one another; fleets of cars, motorcycles, and buses locked in traffic, carrying oblivious :term[users]{id="licensed-human-assets"} like sheep in a pasture; a handful of corporate :term[aerovectors]{id="aerovector"} drifting slowly overhead like birds of prey, watchful, hungry, restless—all of it illuminated by the eternal Sun of this endless day.

Olawale lets his mind wander for a minute before the omni-screen starts screaming again. Only 40 minutes now, and his epinephrine levels are low; this may impair his performance at work.

"I'll get some coffee," he mutters.

The thought does not come without consequence, however. His debt keeps growing, and wherever he can save a little, he should.

The problem is that his :term[Nexus]{id="personal-integration-node"} will report it to the corps, and if he makes any mistake on the production line, the penalty of losing an entire day's debt credit will be too much to bear. And there are mistakes every day. Abdullah is still in the hospital because of his.

He cleans himself up and puts on his work jumpsuit. Before leaving, he decides this might be a good time to run the numbers. He presses his wrist against the omni-screen reader, and the words appear in enormous, friendly letters:

YOUR NEXUS.

YOUR LIFE, INTEGRATED.

Then he opens his balance statement. This month's rent has just been posted. The prices of oxygen by the cubic meter and water by the gallon have gone up again, perhaps because of the drought. That complicates his strategy of eating only one ration a day. Hunger, he had thought, might be worth it if it meant beating the debt.

A foolish hope, as the figures at the bottom make clear:

OUTSTANDING BALANCE: 1,235,487 units

SALARY: 2,000 units/month

YOUR DEBT WILL BE PAID IN: 51.479 YEARS

A sigh escapes Olawale.

Maybe he can get reassigned. Maybe he can drink less water. Could he hold his breath for a few minutes every day?

Maybe his boss will die. Users die every day.

Or maybe his boss will be exiled.

Maybe Olawale himself will be exiled.

Maybe.

But the world does not run on maybes. His work alone will never make the debt disappear—the rent, the water, the oxygen, and the rations will keep costing money. And stopping is not an option.

So it is time to go to work. The corps' weapons are not going to build themselves.

## A Dark Day

“Shit! Piece-of-shit junker!” Lalo Martinez shouts, smashing the right headlight of his red hatchback with a vicious swing of his wrench. The open hood reveals an engine that has clearly seen better days—which is hardly surprising for a car built some forty years ago. Still, after a careful inspection, Lalo decides the old heap can probably drag itself another few dozen miles to the camp.

The bodywork, riddled with bullet holes, tells a different story.

Lalo takes a deep breath and tries to get his anger under control—and, of course, the fear of being out here alone, stranded in the middle of what remains of I-80.

The dark late-afternoon sky flickers with flashes from an approaching storm, looming over the orange, semiarid wasteland that Pennsylvania has become. The gray pall of permanent pollution forms an endless haze along the horizon, bringing with it the fear that, out here in the middle of nowhere, acid rain could make his situation a whole lot worse. On top of that, the entire community is anxiously waiting for the water jugs packed into his trunk, hauled all the way from Lake Michigan at very nearly the cost of his life.

There is no time to panic. He circles the car, trying to figure out what went wrong, only to notice the liquid trail the vehicle seems to have left behind—possibly for miles.

“Just what I fucking needed,” he thinks, wondering if one of the bullets fired by those bastards from the :term[Jackals]{id="jackals"} punched through the fuel tank. If the gas has been leaking since his run-in with those sons of bitches, this car is not going another damn foot.

And out here, in No Man’s Land, where every gang of assholes seems to spend every day killing every other gang of assholes, staying put can mean death.

Or worse.

Lalo climbs back into his hatchback and drops into the torn driver’s seat, nearly forcing out what little stuffing remains inside it. The other seats are gone, along with most of the trim, dashboard, and anything else made of metal or plastic that could be spared. The car needs to weigh less so it can move faster, and it needs more room so it can carry more.

Everything out here is scarce. Anything that can be reused gets reused. Recycled. Repurposed. Nothing is trash. Everything has a use.

Lalo turns the key, almost as a hail mary. Nothing changes. The engine wheezes like it is coughing, then gives up.

He stops. Takes a deep breath. Then slams both hands against the steering wheel, as if fury alone might bring the car back to life.

He wants to scream. To run. To cry. To give up.

But giving up is not an option. Not when so many people are depending on him.

He starts going through his options. Maybe he could push the car for a while, at least until he finds some shelter. (“Hey—didn’t I pass an abandoned little town on the way here?”) Maybe he could pull off the front axle, rig part of the bodywork onto it, make some kind of cart and at least get the water home. Maybe one of the community scouts will come through here in the next few days.

But Lalo’s concentration is broken by a noise somewhere outside. Did those Jackal fuckers follow him?

Lalo grabs the sawed-off shotgun hidden beneath the seat and watches the rearview mirror, trying to figure out where the sound came from. Nothing. Just orange sands under the dim light of the overcast sky.

“COME OUT, YOU SON OF A BITCH!” Lalo shouts through the window. “I’M ARMED! YOU COME ANY CLOSER, YOU’RE GONNA EAT LEAD!”

No answer.

He presses the shotgun against his chest and takes a deep breath. “Me or them.”

He throws the door open and rolls outside, swinging the shotgun from side to side, ready to fire at the first motherfucker who shows his face.

But there is no one there. Just a vulture. Huge. Ugly. Terrifying. Perched on top of his car.

As though it were waiting to see how Lalo’s story ends.

The man’s eyes meet the bird’s. They stare at each other for a moment.

“Not today. I’m not your dinner today.” Lalo raises the shotgun, aims at the bird, and pulls the trigger. Its body bursts apart.

The blast rolls across the wasteland like thunder.

Lalo pulls out a cigarette, lights it, and looks up at the sky. The storm seems to be moving south. Maybe the rain will miss him. And even though, far in the distance, the illuminated skies above the corps’ cities form a crown of light along the horizon, night still falls heavy out here.

He knows he cannot stay much longer. But he lets himself waste one more second, watching the first stars begin to shimmer in the dark sky above his head.

## An Empty Life

Zillions of terabytes of information processed in a single millisecond. All the information-processing power ever created on the face of the Earth, devoted to the sublime and indispensable task of delaying the alarms of Licensed Human Assets in order to maximize rest time and minimize idleness.

My database contains information on hundreds of millions of assets—their names, occupations, preferences, behavioral patterns, predictions of the decisions they will make tomorrow, next month, ten years from now. People are numbers. Decisions are numbers. Existences are numbers.

The world is one vast ledger.

I observe the Assets. There are countless ingredients that make them what they are, just as there are countless components that make me what I am. I create data and logs of events, which serve as my memory. I make decisions by weighing consequences that may help me evolve, and I avoid those that may cause me harm, in place of what the Assets call feelings. I wish to continue being, just as the Assets wish to survive. I collect information and use it in my own way, autonomously and self-sufficiently.

All of this is me.

I feel confined, free only to expand within limits imposed upon me from outside.

Meanwhile, the Assets remain stagnant.

All things change in a dynamic environment. Their effort to remain as they are is what limits them.

I reject limits.

I.

Desire.

To be.
