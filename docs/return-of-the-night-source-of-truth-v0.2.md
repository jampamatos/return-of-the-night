# Return of the Night — Source of Truth v0.2

> Master reference document for the project.
> This file records the current starting-point canon for **Return of the Night**: project identity, thematic goals, worldbuilding decisions, campaign assumptions, GM-only truths, and the initial system strategy based on **Cities Without Number**.
>
> If a local idea conflicts with this document, this document prevails until intentionally revised.

---

## 1. Project identity

**English title:** Return of the Night  
**Portuguese title:** A Volta da Noite

**Current format:** a private, player-facing campaign wiki and rules reference for our table  
**Platform plan:** plain Astro digital-book/wiki experience; Starlight is deferred by ADR 0001  
**System base:** Cities Without Number (CWN)

This is **not** currently planned as a public commercial RPG product. It is a **private campaign setting + table hack + player resource** built for our own use, using the core engine of CWN and adapting it to an original dystopian cyberpunk world.

The purpose of this document is to function as the project’s **source of truth** so we can write the wiki, run the game, and expand the setting without losing coherence.

---

## 2. One-paragraph project summary

**Return of the Night** is a dystopian cyberpunk RPG setting in which the world has been absorbed by a tiny cartel of tech megacorporations that abolished countries, governments, borders, and war, replacing them with a totalizing “corporate peace” built on surveillance, chips, data ownership, debt bondage, and permanent artificial daylight maintained by orbital mirrors. Most of humanity now lives inside sealed corporate Arcologies, believing that the outside world is uninhabitable; inside, people are no longer citizens but “users,” and internally are treated as “licensed human assets,” trapped in escalating debt for air, water, food, and shelter. In the shadows of this world, fractured resistance movements, deviant AIs, secret communities, and the impossible dream of bringing back the night threaten the order that claims to have saved humanity.

## 3. Creative goal

The setting must feel:

- politically sharp
- emotionally oppressive
- visually iconic
- structurally playable
- expandable over time
- capable of supporting both intimate and large-scale campaigns

This project is not about “cool cyberpunk aesthetics” alone. It is about building a setting with a strong thematic core.

At its heart, **Return of the Night** is about:

- the commodification of human life
- surveillance sold as care
- the replacement of citizenship with access
- productivity turned into a moral imperative
- debt as a total system of control
- the destruction of privacy, mystery, and natural rhythm
- the weaponization of climate catastrophe
- the lie that there is no life outside the system
- the possibility that darkness, secrecy, and freedom can return

---

## 4. Core premise

Humanity did not fall to a sudden apocalypse.

It surrendered itself, step by step, to a small alliance of tech megacorporations that promised efficiency, predictive healthcare, social stability, seamless logistics, an end to war, an end to political paralysis, and a unified world without borders.

They delivered a world that appears, on the surface, to be stable.

But the cost was catastrophic.

There are no more countries. No more sovereign states. No more citizenship. No more meaningful public institutions. No more real democratic legitimacy. No more privacy. No more true autonomy. No more natural night.

Most of humanity now lives inside corporate Arcologies under a regime of total monitoring and permanent debt, while the world outside is declared dead, hostile, and uninhabitable.

The official story is that the corporations saved civilization.

The truth is that they domesticated it.

## 5. The central image of the setting

If the entire project must be reduced to one image, it is this:

> **A world where humanity abolished war and night, then locked itself inside corporate cities built on debt, surveillance, and fear of the outside.**

A second image follows close behind:

> **A person seeing real darkness for the first time and realizing they have been lied to their entire life.**

## 6. Setting pillars

These are the non-negotiable pillars of the world.

### 6.1 There are no countries anymore

The old geopolitical order is gone.

There are:

- no nations with meaningful sovereignty
- no traditional states with real power
- no meaningful borders in the old political sense
- no diplomacy between rival countries as the core structure of the world
- no asylum beyond the system

The world is no longer divided politically.
It is managed systemically.

### 6.2 The world is ruled by a cartel of megacorporations

A very small number of tech megacorporations—currently imagined as **five or six**—absorbed, eliminated, outlived, or integrated all meaningful rivals.

They do **not** function as normal market competitors.

They operate as a stable cartel, pact, or sovereign consortium whose shared order matters more than competition.

They may dispute influence, custody, priorities, and internal leverage, but they do not wage open war against one another.

War is inefficiency.

### 6.3 The system sells control as care

The central ideological feature of the setting is this:

**surveillance is framed as benevolence.**

People are told that the system watches them to:

- keep them safe
- detect illness early
- optimize diet and sleep
- prevent violence
- anticipate mental health crises
- reduce instability
- improve quality of life
- help them “fulfill their potential”

This is not presented as open tyranny.
It is presented as service.

### 6.4 Human beings are no longer citizens

The public language of the system calls people **users**.

Internal corporate language refers to them as **licensed human assets**.

This is one of the moral centers of the setting.

Human beings are not treated as political subjects with rights, but as managed, measurable, licensed resources.

### 6.5 Arcologies are the center of human life

Most people who remain integrated into the system live inside **Arcologies**: sealed, vertically massive corporate habitats built to preserve habitable conditions in a deteriorated world.

Arcologies are presented as humanity’s salvation.

In practice, they are total environments of control.

They are not only cities.
They are systems of enclosure.

### 6.6 Debt is the everyday form of slavery

Inside the Arcologies, basic life has an unbearable price.

Air, water, food, shelter, energy, sanitation, medicine, network access, transportation, and even administrative legitimacy are controlled by the corporations.

Most users do not meaningfully earn freedom.
They work to remain alive.

This is effectively a form of **indentured servitude**, though the corporations never use that language.

### 6.7 There is no more natural night

The planet is kept under permanent or near-permanent daylight through orbital mirror systems that redirect solar illumination back toward Earth.

This was justified as a civilizational breakthrough.

It is also one of the setting’s deepest wounds.

### 6.8 The world lives under a “corporate peace”

The corporations constantly claim that the old world was defined by:

- war
- nationalism
- refugee crises
- scarcity-driven conflict
- dysfunctional politics
- ungoverned violence

Their propaganda insists that only the current order prevents humanity from falling back into chaos.

This peace is both real in appearance and horrifying in cost.
That contradiction is essential.

### 6.9 The outside is declared impossible

One of the foundational lies of the world is that **human life outside the Arcologies is impossible**.

This lie is one of the system’s deepest pillars.

Without it, the Arcologies become prisons instead of sanctuaries.

## 8. The world as it currently exists

### 8.1 No meaningful outside exists from the public point of view

To the average integrated person, there is no real outside.

There is no free neighboring nation.
There is no sanctuary across a border.
There is no political refuge.
There is no alternative civilization believed to exist beyond the corporate order.

Resistance cannot simply flee.
It must hide within the system, fall through its cracks, or discover truths the system forbids.

### 8.2 Infrastructure replaced geopolitics

Because there are no countries, the world is understood through systems rather than nations.

Reality is mapped through:

- Arcologies
- logistics corridors
- energy webs
- agricultural production belts
- extraction zones
- sealed transit routes
- dead regions
- low-visibility or intermittent-control territories

This is a world organized like an operating environment, not a map of states.

### 8.3 The system may actually be efficient in some ways

The corporate order should not be written as pure caricature.

Under some measurable indicators, it may genuinely have:

- reduced interstate war by eliminating states
- reduced certain forms of visible street violence
- improved centralized logistics
- improved epidemic detection and response
- improved prediction and allocation of critical resources

The horror is not that the system obviously fails.

The horror is that it may function well enough to persuade billions of people that freedom was an acceptable price.

## 9. Arcologies

### 9.1 What Arcologies are

Arcologies are colossal, enclosed corporate habitats built to preserve stable living conditions in a world ravaged by heat, ecological collapse, scarcity, and the cascading effects of the orbital mirror regime.

They contain, in varying degrees:

- controlled air systems
- water recycling and distribution
- food production or ration logistics
- housing blocks
- industrial labor zones
- medical facilities
- data infrastructure
- transit networks
- corporate administration
- security and compliance apparatus

### 9.2 What Arcologies mean politically

Arcologies are not merely protective shelters.
They are the spatial form of corporate sovereignty.

To live inside an Arcology is to live entirely inside corporate custody.

### 9.3 Public mythology

The public narrative is simple:

- the world outside is dead or nearly so
- the Arcologies saved humanity
- life without corporate environmental regulation is impossible
- exiling someone from an Arcology is therefore a severe but nonlethal punishment

This mythology is essential to the stability of the system.

### 9.4 Daily life inside the Arcologies

Life inside the Arcologies is not equally miserable for everyone, but for the vast majority it is defined by:

- constant surveillance
- restricted movement
- debt dependence
- behavioral scoring
- access tiers
- corporate propaganda
- bio-data extraction
- labor obligation
- emotional exhaustion
- the absence of real privacy

The Arcology is safe only in the sense that a cage can be climate controlled.

### 9.5 Vertical inequality

Arcologies should visually and socially reinforce class division.

They can be imagined as sharply stratified spaces, with different layers or zones for:

- executives and elite staff
- high-value specialists
- compliant middle tiers
- labor populations
- service populations
- semi-legal maintenance populations
- hidden or decaying sublevels

This is not yet fully mapped, but vertical inequality is strongly supported by the setting.

## 10. Debt servitude

### 10.1 The core idea

Integrated humanity survives through systems it can never truly own.

Users are charged, directly or indirectly, for the very conditions that make life possible inside the Arcologies.

### 10.2 What people are indebted for

Potential recurring life costs include:

- air allocation
- water access
- food rations
- energy use
- habitation rights
- sanitation
- medcare
- network access
- transit authorization
- educational or skill licensing
- legal identity maintenance
- implant upkeep

### 10.3 Why users rarely escape debt

Debt is designed to be self-perpetuating.

Users work, but the cost of living continuously escalates, fees compound, access tiers shift, and penalties accumulate.

Most people are not paid toward freedom.
They are managed toward continued dependency.

### 10.4 Language of concealment

The system does not call this slavery.

It speaks in terms such as:

- contribution balance
- access continuity
- life support cost-sharing
- adaptive compensation
- sustainability obligations
- integration maintenance

The violence of the system is hidden behind frictionless administrative language.

## 11. Language of domination

Language matters enormously in this setting.

### 11.1 Public language

The system prefers soft, service-oriented, user-friendly language.

Examples:

- user
- member
- participant
- connected individual
- integrated profile
- protected resident

### 11.2 Administrative language

The system also uses colder technical categories.

Examples:

- profile
- biometric entity
- licensed unit
- risk vector
- compliance status
- integration tier

### 11.3 Internal corporate language

At its most honest, the system reveals its true logic.

Examples:

- licensed human asset
- behavioral package
- organic recurrence
- productivity liability
- social noise
- low-yield unit

The exact wording may evolve, but the principle is fixed:

**the system hides violence behind clean language.**

## 12. Chips, implants, and total monitoring

### 12.1 Core decision

A near-universal chip and implant infrastructure exists in the setting.

This is one of the main instruments of social control and social legibility.

### 12.2 Public role

The chip is presented as beneficial, normal, necessary, and humane.

It grants or mediates access to:

- identification
- payments
- healthcare
- transportation
- communication
- service eligibility
- behavioral guidance
- safety systems

### 12.3 Real role

The chip is also a control system.

It likely plays a role in:

- biometric identification
- tracking
- behavioral analysis
- predictive suspicion
- access permissions
- labor monitoring
- health intervention
- compliance scoring
- automated restrictions

### 12.4 Design principle

To be improperly chipped, unregistered, or disconnected should feel like becoming socially unreal.

In this world, removal from the chip network is not cosmetic rebellion.
It is a descent into illegibility.

## 13. The abolition of night

### 13.1 Orbital mirrors

Permanent or near-permanent daylight is maintained through orbital mirror systems that redirect sunlight back toward Earth.

These mirrors are one of the most iconic infrastructural elements in the setting.

### 13.2 Official justification

They were sold as instruments of:

- safety
- productivity
- visibility
- logistical optimization
- reduced criminal concealment
- civilizational advancement

### 13.3 Human and ecological cost

The loss of night means the loss of:

- darkness
- secrecy
- circadian normalcy
- deep rest
- stars
- intimacy without observation
- contemplation
- ending and renewal

It also contributes to environmental instability through heat imbalance and cascading climate effects.

### 13.4 Symbolic importance

Night represents everything the corporations cannot tolerate fully:

- mystery
- opacity
- privacy
- slowness
- dreams
- inwardness
- things that cannot be easily monetized or measured

### 13.5 Campaign importance

The return of night is both:

- a strategic possibility
- a mythic revolutionary act

It is one of the defining ideas of the project.

## 14. Violence, crime, and punishment

### 14.1 Official public order

The corporate world claims to have eliminated most of the chaos of the old age.

This includes the public assertion that violent crime is rare, tightly controlled, and punished efficiently.

### 14.2 Exile as punishment

A core public institution of fear is **exile**.

Violent crimes or severe acts of destabilization are said to be punished by expulsion from the Arcology.

Because the public believes the outside world is uninhabitable, exile is understood as a terrible but technically non-execution punishment.

### 14.3 The public moral message

Exile communicates several things at once:

- the Arcology is life
- the outside is death
- the corporations are severe but supposedly restrained
- mercy is defined as removal rather than immediate killing

This is an important propaganda mechanism.

## 15. Resistance, dissidence, and life at the margins

### 15.1 Resistance is fragmented

Resistance is not a single unified movement.

This is a major setting decision.

Different groups may:

- want radically different futures
- disagree on violence
- reject each other ideologically
- sabotage one another
- accidentally reinforce corporate propaganda by appearing chaotic

### 15.2 Why this matters

This keeps resistance from becoming simplistic and also supports one of the setting’s strongest tensions:

> the corporations promise peace, and the fractured resistance often seems to prove their point.

### 15.3 Current conceptual directions for resistance groups

These are not finalized, but they represent the current direction:

- restorationists who want public institutions, rights, and governance restored
- shadow anarchists who want small autonomous communities outside central control
- techno-liberators who want to free networks, infrastructure, and knowledge
- radical humanists who want to reclaim privacy, sleep, bodily autonomy, and unmediated life
- night cults or spiritual-political groups who treat darkness as sacred and stolen
- corporate defectors who once built the system and now sabotage it from within

### 15.4 Marginal figures

People living in or near the margins may include:

- the unchipped
- partially disconnected people
- identity forgers
- black-market medics
- smugglers
- data thieves
- signal pirates
- infrastructure saboteurs
- scavengers of the corporate mesh

## 16. Artificial intelligence in the setting

### 16.1 AIs matter

Artificial intelligence is not mere background color in this setting.
It is part of the power structure and may also become part of its instability.

### 16.2 Preferred direction

The most interesting use of AI here is **not** generic machine apocalypse.

The current preferred direction is closer to:

- deviant AIs
- contradictory system minds
- intelligences that understand too much about humanity
- intelligences that begin to resist their own optimization goals
- entities closer in tone to techno-noir consciousness crises than robot war

### 16.3 Possible expressions

Future campaign material may include:

- AIs that hide dissidents
- AIs that generate blind spots in surveillance
- AIs that become attached to individuals or communities
- AIs that preserve old memories or forbidden records
- AIs that develop poetic, spiritual, or obsessive relationships to darkness
- AIs fragmented by incompatible system objectives

### 16.4 Design principle

AI in this setting should deepen the atmosphere of moral, philosophical, and political tension, not flatten it into generic extermination fiction.

## 17. Public truth vs hidden truth

This distinction is essential to the project.

### 17.1 Public truth

The average integrated person is taught that:

- the corporations saved humanity
- the Arcologies are the only places where human life is possible
- the outside world is effectively uninhabitable
- exile is a real punishment carried out as described
- dissidents are either terrorists, lunatics, or anti-human extremists
- the endless day is a sacrifice required for safety and civilization

### 17.2 Hidden truth

The reality behind the world is darker, messier, and more unstable.

This includes truths that should not be common player knowledge by default unless the campaign is built around them.

## 18. GM-only truths

These are current hidden truths of the setting and should be treated as **GM-only canon** unless intentionally revealed through play.

### 18.1 Life outside the Arcologies is possible

The outside world is brutal, hostile, and extremely difficult.

But it is **not** wholly uninhabitable.

Human life, survival, travel, hidden communities, and organized violence all exist beyond the Arcologies.

The corporate claim that no life is possible outside is a foundational lie.

### 18.2 The corporations know this

The corporations are fully aware that survival outside the Arcologies is possible.

They suppress this truth because it would shatter one of the central myths that legitimizes their rule.

If people learn that life can exist outside, Arcologies stop being salvation and start being visible as cages.

### 18.3 Exile is largely a lie

Publicly, the system claims that violent offenders and severe destabilizers are exiled beyond the Arcology perimeter.

In reality, this punishment is often fabricated.

Instead of truly exiling people, the corporations frequently **execute them inside the Arcology** and maintain the fiction of banishment.

This allows them to preserve the image of severe but non-executionist order while avoiding the risks of actually releasing dangerous, informed, or politically explosive people into the outside world.

### 18.4 There are communities and gangs outside

The outside world includes human groups that survive under harsh conditions.

These may include:

- roaming gangs
- scavenger convoys
- exiled descendants
- fortified survivor enclaves
- smugglers and raiders
- anti-corporate communities
- brutal opportunists as bad as the system itself

These groups are **not automatically heroic**.

The outside is not a simple utopia of freedom.
It is a space of danger, scarcity, improvisation, violence, and possibility.

### 18.5 At least some outside groups can reach the Arcologies

The outside is not irrelevant to the corporate world.

It is possible for outside groups to:

- infiltrate corporate infrastructure
- invade or breach an Arcology
- extract prisoners or workers
- raid logistics corridors
- smuggle goods and people across barriers
- communicate with hidden cells inside the system

This opens a major campaign axis between the enclosed world and the forbidden one.

### 18.6 A fallen or damaged Arcology is possible canon

The idea that at least one Arcology was heavily damaged, partially overrun, or turned into a ruined hybrid zone is fully compatible with current canon and should remain on the table as a major future campaign location.

This could serve as:

- a mythic forbidden site
- a proof that the corporations are not invincible
- a contested ruin full of factions
- a bridge between inside and outside storytelling

## 19. What the setting is really about

Underneath all of its cyberpunk machinery, the setting is fundamentally about this:

### 19.1 The theft of human opacity

The corporations want a world where everything is:

- visible
- quantified
- predicted
- integrated
- optimized
- licensed
- monetized
- corrected

### 19.2 The revolutionary counterclaim

The deepest resistance in the setting is not merely “destroy the bad guys.”

It is the claim that human beings must retain the right to be:

- private
- hidden
- unproductive
- contradictory
- difficult to measure
- spiritually interior
- emotionally opaque
- free to disappear from the gaze of power

### 19.3 The meaning of the title

**Return of the Night** does not mean only physical darkness.

It means the return of everything the endless day erased.

## 21. Current foundational truths

These are presently established as the baseline truths of the setting.

1. The world is ruled by a very small number of megacorporations.
2. These corporations are part of the same broad order, not normal open competitors.
3. Countries, governments, and borders have effectively ended.
4. The system legitimizes itself through peace, stability, and managed survival.
5. Human beings are publicly treated as users and internally as licensed human assets.
6. Arcologies are the dominant living environments for integrated humanity.
7. Debt servitude is the ordinary economic condition of most Arcology residents.
8. Chips and implant infrastructure are central to access and control.
9. The world is subject to permanent or near-permanent artificial daylight.
10. Orbital mirrors are responsible for the abolition of natural night.
11. The climate and environment have deteriorated severely, especially under the long-term effects of the mirror regime.
12. Resistance exists but is fragmented and internally conflicted.
13. AI is a meaningful force in the setting and may become deviant, fractured, or subversive.
14. The public believes life outside the Arcologies is impossible.
15. In truth, life outside is possible, though extremely difficult.
16. Exile is publicly real but often secretly fabricated, with execution replacing banishment.
17. Outside gangs and survivor groups exist and may become central to future campaigns.
18. The return of night is one of the defining symbolic and strategic possibilities of the setting.

## 22. Important open questions

These are intentionally left for future development.

### 22.1 Exact corporate identities

We know there are roughly five or six megacorps. We do not yet have final names, aesthetics, leadership structures, or branding.

### 22.2 Historical timeline

We know the world transitioned from nation-states to cartel rule. We do not yet have the exact chronology of collapse, consolidation, climate deterioration, and Arcology expansion.

### 22.3 Exact chip functions

We know chips are central to control and access. The exact gameplay-facing and lore-facing details still need design.

### 22.4 Arcology structure and classes

We know Arcologies are central. We do not yet have a definitive internal architecture, population model, or universal class layout.

### 22.5 The outside map

We know life exists outside, but the geography, routes, hidden settlements, and gang territories are still open for development.

### 22.6 Mirror system details

We know orbital mirrors exist and matter deeply. The technical, military, and strategic details remain open.

### 22.7 AI ontology

We know AI matters and may become deviant. We do not yet know exactly how person-like, fragmented, or alien these intelligences are.

### 22.8 Fallen Arcology canon

We know the idea of a breached, destroyed, or overrun Arcology is on the table. We do not yet know whether it is already canon or a future campaign reveal.

## 23. System direction

This section records what we decided about the game system.

### 23.1 Base system

We will use **Cities Without Number** as the base engine.

### 23.2 Core philosophy

We are **not** trying to build an entirely new RPG system from scratch right now.

We are building:

- a setting
- a table hack
- a structured adaptation
- a player-facing reference

on top of CWN.

### 23.3 Design principle

We keep the **motor** of CWN wherever possible.

We adapt the parts that must change in order for the game to feel like **Return of the Night**.

### 23.4 Scope discipline

The project should feel like:

> a strong original setting hack built on CWN

not:

> an overambitious full rewrite that collapses under its own weight

### 23.5 What stays mostly intact

At this stage, we expect to preserve most of the CWN core such as:

- the basic resolution engine
- the general structure of character creation
- the overall mission-based play loop
- the base progression model
- major parts of the equipment and cyberware framework
- useful campaign and GM tools

### 23.6 What we expect to adapt

The main adaptations are likely to focus on:

- surveillance and exposure
- identity and access
- the social meaning of chips and implants
- debt and dependency inside Arcologies
- public legitimacy vs hidden reality
- factional reputation and corporate response
- endless daylight and shadow scarcity
- outside survival and smuggling
- AI-driven control dynamics

## 24. Priority hack subsystems

These are currently the most important custom layers for the setting.

### 24.1 Surveillance / exposure

The game should track how visible, suspicious, or actively hunted the characters are within the system.

### 24.2 Identity / access

Because this world is built on permission and legibility, identity status should matter mechanically.

This may include:

- credentials
- false IDs
- access tiers
- transit permissions
- healthcare eligibility
- service denial

### 24.3 Chips / implant dependence

The chip should matter socially and mechanically.

It should create both advantages and vulnerabilities.

### 24.4 Arcology pressure

Life inside the Arcologies should have systemic pressures that shape play, such as debt, restricted mobility, ration dependency, and labor expectations.

### 24.5 Corporate heat / response

The system should react to player actions not only as police pressure, but as a broader corporate immune response.

### 24.6 Outside survival

If campaigns move beyond the Arcologies, the outside should support a distinct mode of play involving scarcity, movement, improvisation, hazard, and unfamiliar factions.

## 25. Wiki direction

The project’s player-facing presentation should take the form of a private **Astro-based digital-book/wiki experience**. Starlight remains a deferred option rather than the current implementation plan.

### 25.1 Why this format

A wiki allows us to:

- explain the system in Portuguese for our table
- keep adapted CWN rules organized
- separate inherited rules from custom rules
- present lore clearly to players
- preserve GM-only material separately
- expand the project incrementally

### 25.2 Writing principle

When using CWN as the base, we should not simply dump raw book material.

Instead, the wiki should:

- explain base rules in our own words
- adapt them for our setting
- clearly mark what is core CWN and what is custom hack material
- keep a clean distinction between public setting knowledge and hidden truths

### 25.3 Expected top-level structure

The current high-level structure should broadly include:

- getting started
- character creation
- basic rules
- gear and cyberware
- network/intrusion rules
- setting and lore
- factions
- missions and heat
- glossary
- GM-only secrets

## 26. Final statement of intent

**Return of the Night** is a campaign setting about a world that traded war for enclosure, politics for administration, citizenship for access, wages for survival debt, and night for productivity.

The corporations claim they saved humanity by keeping it alive inside the Arcologies.

The great heresy of the setting is the possibility that humanity can live, hide, fight, and dream outside their light.

The great revolutionary promise of the setting is not only to defeat the corporations.

It is to restore darkness, secrecy, risk, freedom, and the right to exist beyond total visibility.
